02About

Hardware owned end to end.

I build the electronics that sit between a physical signal and a decision. Right now that means radar sensing for defence — compact, high-frequency sensors that have to detect and track small, fast-moving aerial targets reliably.

The role is deliberately end to end. It starts at requirements and trade studies, runs through detection and link-budget analysis and antenna design backed by EM simulation, and carries on into real hardware: RF and mixed-signal boards, digital back-end sizing, power architecture and the safety side of the system.

A lot of it is systems engineering rather than any single discipline — reconciling what neighbouring teams hand over against what the sensor can physically do, and closing the gaps that turn up.

I came to it from acoustic counter-UAS: microphone arrays, the firmware that reads them, and the maths that turns a few microseconds of delay into a bearing. When the off-the-shelf path ran out I built my own — the classic ESP32 gives you one I2S RX channel, so I moved capture to TDM and built the rig that validated the custom arrays single-handedly.
